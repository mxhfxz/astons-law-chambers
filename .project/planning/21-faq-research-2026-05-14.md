# FAQ research — "Will I go to prison?" / "Will I have a record?"

Written 2026-05-14. Plain notes. No copy here — copy gets written only after
Ghulam verifies the factors below.

The questions defendants actually ask are some of the highest-trust moments
available on a criminal defence site. They are also the most dangerous to
write generically, because the truthful answer is "it depends on the following
things" and getting "the following things" wrong is a real problem on a legal
site.

This note lists the factors that drive the answer in each of the three areas
we proposed FAQs for, so you can either confirm them or correct them before
copy is written.

---

## Where the answers come from in UK criminal sentencing

For any criminal charge in England and Wales, the sentence (or whether there
is one at all) is driven by a small set of inputs:

1. **The offence and its statutory maximum.** Sets the ceiling.
2. **The Sentencing Council guideline for that offence.** A two-axis grid:
   harm (how serious the consequences were) by culpability (how blameworthy
   the conduct was). The cell produces a starting point and a range.
3. **Aggravating and mitigating factors.** Move the sentence within the
   range, or take it outside.
4. **Previous convictions.** Treated as a statutory aggravating factor.
5. **Plea credit.** A guilty plea at the first opportunity gets up to a third
   off; later pleas get less.
6. **The custody threshold.** The court has to decide the offence is so
   serious that only custody can be justified. If it crosses, prison is on
   the table; if not, community order or fine is the route.
7. **Suspended sentence considerations.** Even if a custodial sentence is
   passed, it can be suspended (typically up to 2 years, with conditions).
8. **Totality.** Where there are multiple offences, the total sentence has
   to be just and proportionate overall.

The "record" question is separate from the prison question:

- A conviction stays on the Police National Computer indefinitely (subject to
  rehabilitation periods that change what employers can ask about).
- A caution is also a record, just a different kind.
- Out-of-court disposals (community resolution, conditional caution) still
  appear on enhanced DBS checks for the relevant period.

## Per-area factor framework

### Criminal Defence (general / catch-all FAQ)

**For "Will I go to prison?":**
- The offence charged and its custody threshold.
- Sentencing guideline harm-and-culpability cell.
- Previous convictions (or none).
- Aggravating factors specific to the case (weapon, vulnerability, breach of
  trust, group offending).
- Mitigating factors (genuine remorse, cooperation, age, mental health,
  personal circumstances).
- Plea timing.
- Availability of suspension (sentence length, eligibility, current sentence
  being served).

**For "Will I have a record?":**
- Whether the matter results in a conviction, caution, or no further action.
- Whether police choose an out-of-court disposal (community resolution,
  fixed penalty, conditional caution).
- Whether the offence is recordable (most are, some summary-only minor ones
  are not).

**Flag for Ghulam to verify before any copy is written:**
- Are there specific charge categories where you'd want to caveat differently
  (e.g. terrorism / serious organised crime where the guideline starting
  points are very high)?
- How would you frame the answer for clients with prior convictions? The
  honest answer changes substantially.

### Violent Crimes (assault, ABH, GBH)

The Sentencing Council guideline for assault offences is the primary anchor.
It works on:

**Harm categories** (Category 1 most serious to Category 3 least):
- Extent of injury (physical and psychological).
- Whether the victim was particularly vulnerable.
- Sustained or repeated assault.
- Use of a weapon or weapon-equivalent.

**Culpability categories** (A high / B medium / C lesser):
- Intent vs. recklessness.
- Use of weapon (separately weighted).
- Targeting (race, religion, sexual orientation, vulnerability).
- Group offending or leading role.
- Provocation or fear.

**The grid cell** (e.g. Cat 1A vs Cat 3C) produces a starting point that
ranges from a community order at the bottom end of common assault to many
years' custody at the top end of GBH with intent (which is itself capped at
life).

**For "Will I go to prison?":**
- The grid cell from the guideline.
- Whether the offence is summary-only (common assault), either-way (ABH,
  s20 GBH), or indictable-only (s18 GBH with intent).
- Whether the matter is allocated to magistrates or Crown Court.
- Plea timing.
- Previous convictions, especially for violence.

**For "Will I have a record?":**
- Any conviction for assault is a recordable conviction.
- The rehabilitation period after sentence varies by sentence type — a
  community order's rehabilitation period is different from a custodial
  sentence's, and enhanced DBS shows convictions and certain cautions
  indefinitely for some roles.

**Flag for Ghulam to verify:**
- Whether to mention specific section numbers (s39 CJA, s47 OAPA, s20 OAPA,
  s18 OAPA) in plain client-facing copy. Per memory `feedback_legal_specificity`
  the default is to soften statutory references.
- Whether to mention "self-defence" as a distinct route — defendants commonly
  ask about this and it can change the answer entirely.

### Drug Offences (possession, supply, importation)

The Sentencing Council drug offences guideline drives this. It works on
two axes that are different from the violence guideline:

**Role** (leading / significant / lesser):
- Leading role: directs activity, substantial commercial profit, links to
  the chain of supply.
- Significant role: operates under direction but with awareness of scale,
  some commercial benefit.
- Lesser role: limited function, performed under direction, no commercial
  benefit, no awareness of scale.

**Quantity bands**, by drug class:
- Class A: thresholds for indicative quantity (e.g. heroin, cocaine, MDMA).
- Class B: cannabis, ketamine, amphetamine, etc.
- Class C: less common in defence work but covered.

**The grid cell** (role × quantity × class) produces starting points that
range from a community order for low-quantity lesser-role Class B possession
through to many years' custody for high-quantity leading-role Class A
supply or importation.

**For "Will I go to prison?":**
- Role assessment is often the most contested element. Disclosure and
  forensic evidence (encrypted messaging, financial analysis, drug analysis)
  drive role determination.
- Quantity and purity — quantity at street-deal level moves the case very
  differently from kilo-scale.
- Personal use vs. intent to supply. Possession with intent and supply are
  treated very differently.
- Whether the case crosses the custody threshold at all (the lowest
  cells of the grid for cannabis possession often don't).
- Whether the defendant was acting under coercion or modern slavery — a
  full defence in some cases (s45 Modern Slavery Act).

**For "Will I have a record?":**
- All drug offences (other than the rare unrecorded ones) create a
  recordable record on conviction.
- Cannabis warnings and community resolutions for personal-use possession
  still appear on enhanced DBS.

**Flag for Ghulam to verify:**
- Whether the FAQ should distinguish personal-use from supply explicitly,
  given how different the answer is.
- How to phrase the "role" point in plain client-facing language without
  inviting clients to self-classify wrongly before instruction.
- Modern slavery / coercion as a flagged route — useful to mention or too
  technical for the FAQ?

## How this maps to FAQ writing

Three things follow from the framework above for how the FAQ copy should be
shaped. None of these is the FAQ copy itself — copy gets written after
Ghulam confirms the factors above.

1. **Each FAQ should answer "depends on X, Y, Z" not "yes/no".** A "yes"
   reads as a guarantee. A "no" reads as a lawyer over-promising. "It
   depends on the following things, here is how those things work in
   practice" is the honest position and the trust-builder.

2. **The X, Y, Z list should be short and concrete.** Three to five factors
   per FAQ. The factors should be what the client can identify or describe
   without legal training — "what was the level of injury?", "is there a
   previous conviction?", "what role were you said to be in?" — not
   "what category of harm under the guideline?".

3. **Each FAQ should close with a next-step sentence.** Not a sales line.
   Something like "the right answer for your case turns on the specific
   facts — the first call covers them" or similar, in your voice. The point
   is to take the reader from "the answer is uncertain" to "calling makes
   the uncertainty resolvable" without it sounding like a pitch.
