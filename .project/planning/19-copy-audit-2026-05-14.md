## Audience and KPI frame used

**Reader state:** Defendant in crisis, or the family member calling on their
behalf. Two dominant emotional modes:

- Emergency state: in custody, hearing imminent, just been charged. High panic,
  very short attention, single goal — find someone now.
- Deliberation state: case in the diary, comparing barristers, evaluating fees.
  Anxious but cognitively functional. Asking is this person credible, can I
  afford them, what happens next.

**Awareness stage:** Most arrive problem-aware (knows they need legal help) or
solution-aware (knows they need a barrister). Very few are unaware.

**Category trust:** Low by default. Legal services are opaque, high-stakes, and
the reader has no good way to evaluate quality before they spend.

**KPI:** Phone, then WhatsApp, then cal.com, in that order.

## What is already working — preserve

These mechanisms are pulling weight. Do not remove or weaken them as a
side-effect of any P-item:

1. Loss-frame anchor "before the interview starts/begins" — repeats across
   police banner, emergency strip, mega-menu CTA, practice-area police banner.
2. "The first call is free" — repeats across hero (implicit), booking module,
   "Before you call" callout, asides.
3. "Booking does not imply instruction" — regret-aversion mechanism.
4. "Before you call" callout on practice-area asides — best CRO mechanism on the
   site. The reader is told what a useful call covers, then given explicit
   permission to call without all of it.
5. Voice-of-customer FAQs on Drug Offences and Driving Offences. Examples:
   "I'm over the limit — is there any defence?", "Will I lose my job if I
   lose my licence?", "I've been told to expect a long sentence — is that
   right?"
6. Sticky pill with availability dot and tel chip showing the number directly.

## Prioritised recommendations (P1–P10)

By estimated KPI impact. Highest first.

### P1 — Home hero rotation toward reader-state plus add WhatsApp CTA

The current hero is firm-described:

> H1: "Defence representation, direct from the barrister."
> Lead: "Astons Law Chambers acts in criminal defence — from the police station
> through to trial and appeal. Instructions are taken direct, by phone or
> WhatsApp. Available 24/7 for police station support."

It is solution-aware copy aimed at a reader who already knows what direct access
means and is comparing barristers. Most arrivals are problem-aware. The
emergency strip below the hero does the reader-state work but it is the second
screen on mobile.

The hero also offers only one CTA (Call). WhatsApp first appears on the booking
module as a tertiary "Prefer to message?" link. For the deliberation-state
reader (on the train, at work, kids in the room), call is the wrong channel
and WhatsApp would convert them.

Suggested direction (not a final rewrite — the user picks the wording):

- Keep H1 "Defence representation, direct from the barrister." Calm,
  entity-correct.
- Rotate the lead paragraph to reader-state first. Example: "Whatever stage the
  case is at — police station, first hearing, trial date set, conviction to
  appeal — chambers acts in criminal defence and takes instructions direct.
  Available 24/7 for police station support."
- Add a secondary WhatsApp button beside Call. Call = btn-xl btn-primary,
  WhatsApp = btn-lg btn-secondary or btn-whatsapp. Sticky-pill availability
  language can be carried into the WhatsApp button's aria-label.

Highest single-change KPI lift on the site.

### P2 — Practice-area `situation` paragraphs rotated to reader-state-first

Seven pages, small changes, consistent pattern. Each currently opens with a
firm-described scope sentence. Rotating the first sentence to reader-state
widens engagement on the page that does the conversion work.

Per-area notes:

- Criminal Defence: already reader-state-led ("From arrest to verdict, chambers
  acts for the defence at every stage"). Leave.
- Violent Crimes: firm-described. Possible lead: "An allegation of assault, ABH,
  or GBH creates pressure long before the trial date — bail conditions, work,
  family. Chambers acts at the police station, through Magistrates' and Crown
  Court."
- Youth Crimes: firm-described. Audience is almost always the parent. The FAQ
  "My child has been arrested — what do I do first?" already carries the
  parent-state. Possible tighten of situation paragraph to match.
- Driving Offences: firm-described. The reader's frame is licence and
  livelihood. Possible lead: "A driving charge often comes down to one question
  — will the licence go? Chambers acts on drink and drug driving, totting-up,
  exceptional-hardship arguments, and disqualification work."
- Drug Offences: firm-described. Possible lead: anchor on the disclosure-driven
  defence point that is currently in the contextual aside ("The schedules
  drive the defence.").
- Appeals: firm-described and time-bounded. Lead with the deadline: "Appeal
  time runs from the day of the decision, and the window is short. Chambers
  advises on prospects, drafts grounds, and represents in the Crown Court and
  Court of Appeal."
- Inquests: family-state-relevant but flat. Possible lead: "Coroner's
  proceedings rarely move at the family's pace. Scope is set at the
  pre-inquest review, and that is the hearing position matters most."

### P3 — Add a "Will I go to prison?" FAQ slot to Criminal Defence, Violent Crimes, Drug Offences

These are the questions defendants are actually asking. Real, plain answers
(which by their nature have to be "depends, and here is what it depends on")
are one of the highest trust acts available on the site.

Candidate questions to cover at least one of, per area:

- "Will I go to prison?"
- "Will I have a criminal record?"
- "Will this be reported anywhere?"
- "Will I have to go to court?"

Some answers may need a 🚩 flag for user verification before publishing.

### P4 — Replicate the "Before you call" callout on the home page and the police-station landing page

Currently confined to practice-area asides. The mechanism is the highest-CRO
density on the site. Worth replicating in compact form near the home-hero CTA
and on the police-station landing page (which is adjacent but does not
currently carry the same content).

Pair P4 with P1 as the first ship.

### P5 — Tighten the H2s carrying legal jargon

- "Instructing the chambers directly" → "How to instruct chambers directly", or
  "Getting a barrister without a solicitor" (plain English).
- "The cases chambers takes" → "Cases chambers defends" (agency-forward verb).
- "Speak to someone today" → "Speak to the barrister today" (names the role
  the reader is buying).
- "Process" → "What happens after you call" (defendant-aware sequence).
- Direct Access H1 "Direct Access" → "Instructing a barrister directly" with
  "Direct Access" as eyebrow.

### P6 — Card sub-lines on mega menu and homepage grid: pair offence categories with what chambers actually does

Same word count, more action. Examples:

- Violent Crimes: "Assault, ABH, GBH. Police-station through appeal."
- Drug Offences: "Possession, supply, importation. Forensic and disclosure
  challenges."

### P7 — About page: surface the CPS-prosecution-experience line

Lift "The practice has experience of prosecution work, having been instructed
by the Crown Prosecution Service." from buried paragraph 2 to its own short
beat early on the page. This is the single strongest credibility line on the
About page for a sophisticated reader and is currently quiet.

Also combine Geography and Address sections into one H2 ("Where chambers
practises") to reduce fragmentation. Possible H1 upgrade: "About Astons Law
Chambers" (entity-first, matches eyebrow pattern elsewhere).

### P8 — Decide "chambers" vs "the chambers" vs "Astons Law Chambers"

No correct answer. There is a deliberate tradeoff between the entity-first
rule (memory) and the natural-voice rule (memory). The two pull in opposite
directions on this exact word.

Options:

- Keep "chambers" as-is. Defensible, tradition-correct, signals the chambers
  takes itself seriously.
- Move to "the chambers" consistently. Slightly more readable for cold visitors.
- Move to "Astons Law Chambers" in body copy, reserving "chambers" for second
  references. Most plain-English, slightly more brand-repetition.

User decision. Consistency thereafter.

### P9 — Header CTA "Book a consultation" → "Book a call"

Matches the rest of the site's language. Lower friction than "consultation"
which reads as professional-services jargon. Low risk.

### P10 — Footer WhatsApp link

"WhatsApp chambers" → "Message on WhatsApp" (matches CTA elsewhere). Consider
giving the link the brand green pill treatment so WhatsApp has actual visual
presence in the footer. WhatsApp is KPI #2 and currently has no visual weight
there.
